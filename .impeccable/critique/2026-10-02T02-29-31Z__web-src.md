## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** Grounded in the product at the level of content, copy and behaviour; generic at the level of shell and visual language. Only this product could have: the first-run guide (the real connection string, a prompt built from the visitor's own schema, a page that listens for the real client and shows its first call as a log row), the grant picker's consequence sentence, result-format cards showing the wire format, `:name` placeholders tinted the same everywhere, the database pinned in the sidebar. Any admin tool could use the shell unchanged: grey sidebar, floating white panel, Inter 13px, one blue, pill badges, a KPI strip and two bar charts. For an Operate surface the specificity sits where it should; the Overview is the least specific page, and the access pages are less specific than the idea they carry.

**Deterministic scan.** CLI scan of `web/src`: 0 findings. In the browser (five pages, overlay shown): 38 findings, of which 4 are real: an 11px stat hint (`components/parts.tsx`), the amber "Denied" figure at 3.2:1 (`components/parts.tsx`), the logs table overflowing its container by 22px at 1200px (`pages/LogsPage.tsx`), and Inter as the only UI typeface. False positives: all 18 "nested cards" (the page frame and panel header bars are not cards), the shell's shadow (the rule ignores its negative spread), one cramped-padding hit on a segmented toggle, two overstated line lengths. The detector missed everything the review found about focus, responsiveness and state, which is what a pattern scan would be expected to miss.

## Overall Impression

A calm, well-made instrument panel whose best moment is the first call arriving in the guide. It loses points on control, efficiency and help rather than on craft. The single biggest opportunity: make risk and failure legible where decisions are made, since "decide exactly what a client may do to your database" is the pitch.

## What's Working

1. The guide teaches by doing, on the real thing: finishing it means the product actually works.
2. Access copy states consequences ("For a database you would hand a psql prompt to"), and writes re-arm on every visit.
3. Numbers are treated as the product: tabular mono, one hue for volume, reserved outcome colours, a table view of the chart.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] The first-run guide disappeared mid-flow and could not be reopened.** It was shown only while no key had been used, and a client marks its key used on connecting, before any call. The curl tab could never complete it. Fix: remember that the guide began, add a way back, make curl call a tool, keep the key in reach after the first call. Command: `/impeccable onboard`. **Fixed** in the commit before the design pass.
- **[P1] Below 768px the two workbenches are unusable and every page starts with 440px of navigation.** The editor measures 5px tall; the console's top bar overflows; rank bars collapse. Half a laptop screen beside a terminal is under 768px. Fix: a real editor height, a collapsing sidebar, wrapping actions. Command: `/impeccable adapt`.
- **[P1] Keyboard and screen-reader users lose their place.** Drawers never take focus, modals do not trap it, neither has a name or returns focus; rows have a near-invisible focus state; errors are not announced; the title never changes. Command: `/impeccable harden`.
- **[P2] Risk and failure are not legible where decisions are made.** A key holding admin tools looks like a read-only one; dangerous groups are unmarked until ticked; errors are amber, the colour of "withheld" and of the armed write switch; the console's hint for a blocked write points nowhere; failures use the success toast. Command: `/impeccable clarify`.
- **[P2, with one P1 bug] Irreversible moments are under-guarded.** A link in the editor's rail leaves a dirty draft without asking; the one-time key dialog closes on Escape; "Clear" deletes the whole log and its statistics from beside "Live". Command: `/impeccable harden`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user).** No time filter or shareable URL in Logs; no next/previous in drawers; no filter on Tools; only two shortcuts with 10px hints; every destructive action is a blocking native confirm; console results cannot be copied.

**Sam (keyboard and screen reader).** Everything in the third issue; the Logs "table" is div grids; placeholder text is the only label on the tool name and description; the idle note that states the safety guarantee is about 2.4:1; disabled and enabled keys differ by one small badge.

**Jordan (first-timer).** "Client" was never explained; disabled primary buttons give no reason; three Copy buttons in step three with advice that cancelled itself; nothing said what to do if the client never connected; no help link after the guide.

## Minor Observations

Copy: params vs arguments; Caller vs API key; denied vs refused; "admin" means four things; raw enum values in the log drawer; "0 ms" and "0%" on an empty overview; "26.6k"; "22 ms" vs "15ms"; "Clear" vs "Clear filters".
Visual: titles printed twice; stat hint off its neighbours' baseline; the Callers card stretched; mixed radii; raw Tailwind colours beside tokens; backdrop blur.
States: the whole app replaced by a spinner on lazy pages; the sidebar key count stale; live tail pauses silently; validation fires on the first keystroke; the setup-code error far from its field.
Responsive: Logs detail column off-screen at 900px; touch targets of 32px and a 24x14px switch.
Other: fonts load from Google Fonts, a third-party request from a self-hosted admin tool.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the promise is "decide exactly what a client may do", why is the API keys page a list of names with grey chips rather than a picture of what each client can read, change and destroy?
2. Should the admin's own console calls count in the Overview at all, or is the Overview about what clients do?
3. What would the tool editor be if it started from the question a client will ask, rather than a blank sheet and two unlabelled inputs?
4. Does the shell need one thing that says "this is a live database with clients attached", or is the quiet frame the point?
